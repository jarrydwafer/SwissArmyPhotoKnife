How this was measured

The phone's lens has a fixed aperture. With the exposure locked, the page knows exactly how much light the sensor was given, so the brightness of the metered area tells it how bright the scene is.

N
Phone lens aperture (fixed, set in Setup)
t
Phone shutter time in seconds, as the camera reports it
S
Phone ISO, as the camera reports it
Y
Average brightness of the metered area, converted back to linear light (0–1)
0.18
Mid-grey: a meter assumes the scene averages out to 18% grey
k
Response slope from the linearity test (1 = perfectly linear)
C
Calibration offset against your camera's meter

Then for your film: EV = EV100 + log₂(ISO/100) and for each aperture t = N² / 2EV.

What we're finding out

Can this phone lock the exposure? Does brightness follow the exposure? Does it report live auto-exposure values? How far off is it from a real meter? Run 2 and 3, do a few calibration points, then share the report.

Fill the view with an evenly lit, plain surface (a wall, a grey card, a sheet of paper in shade). Hold still. The page steps the shutter, then the ISO, and checks that brightness follows: one stop more light should double it.

Switches to auto exposure for 10 seconds. During that time, point at a bright window, then at a dark corner, then back. The app checks whether the camera reports the shutter and ISO it picks.
